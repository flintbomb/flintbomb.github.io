ARmesh

Adventure Radar Mesh. ARmesh pairs an Android phone with an ARmesh node over Bluetooth and shows where the rest of your group is on a topographic map, in country with no phone signal at all. The radios carry the positions; the phone is the screen.

  • Privacy policy

What it does

  • Map: everyone in your group, on a topographic map that keeps working offline from tiles already cached.
  • Compass: an arrow and a distance to the person you are heading for, so you can follow it with the phone in front of you.
  • Groups: up to ten people, formed by scanning a QR code while you are together, or admitted over the radio afterwards.
  • Alarms: ask your own group for assistance, or raise an emergency that reaches everyone in range — including the people who cannot normally see you.
  • Node setup: name your node, set it public or private, and update its firmware over the same Bluetooth link.

What you need

  • An Android phone running Android 8.0 or newer.
  • An ARmesh node for each person, and no mobile coverage required for any of it.

What it is not

ARmesh is not an emergency service and not a substitute for one. An emergency alarm reaches other ARmesh nodes within radio range; it does not reach 911, a dispatcher, or any rescue organisation, and nothing guarantees that a node is in range to hear it. Carry a personal locator beacon or a satellite messenger if you need to reach help that is not in your group.

Positions travel between nodes unencrypted, so anyone in range with compatible hardware can receive them. The privacy policy says exactly what goes over the air.
